## Caption
Every BMW M engine has a signature. Literally.

In a small Bavarian factory, one technician assembles each M engine from first bolt to final test—400 hours per engine. When they're done, their name goes on a metal plate on the valve cover.

No assembly lines. No handoffs. Just 1 person, 1 engine, 1 promise.

This tradition started in 1972 with the first M1 prototype. 50+ years later, BMW still refuses to automate it.

Why? Because a machine can't feel when a component fits perfectly. A craftsman can.

What you feel when you drive a BMW M:
→ 50/50 weight distribution (balanced like a scalpel since 1962)
→ Rear-wheel drive soul (even in their SUVs, the rear wheels lead)
→ An engine assembled by someone who'll never meet you, but built it like they would

The blue-and-white roundel isn't a logo. It's a handshake between an engineer in Bavaria and your right foot.

Ever driven something hand-built? Tag a BMW owner who gets it. 👇
