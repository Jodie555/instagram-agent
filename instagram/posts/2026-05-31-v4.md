## Caption
Imagine this: You’re stepping into a living painting. The cool morning air carries the faint sweetness of azaleas, and beneath your feet, stones slick with dew lead you into Nezu Shrine’s iconic azalea tunnel. It’s just you, a quiet moment, and the warm reds, pinks, and whites of 3,000 blooming shrubs arching overhead. 🌸

Nestled in Tokyo’s historic Bunkyo neighborhood, Nezu Shrine has been a hub for spring pilgrimages since the Edo Period (1603–1868). Wander these gardens, part of the shrine’s Otome Inari area, and you’ll find a delicate balance of nature and tradition that feels worlds away from the city’s rush. Built in 1706, the shrine still carries whispers of its 300-year-old heritage.
